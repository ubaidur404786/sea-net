\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Research questions.}
\textbf{RQ1} Does the small model retain classification accuracy? \textbf{RQ2} Does it retain a
useful interpretation? \textbf{RQ3} How much cost is actually removed? \textbf{RQ4} What does the
bottleneck contribute? \textbf{RQ5} What does \mbox{Top-$k$} pooling contribute?

\paragraph{Datasets.}
\webtraffic{} is the synthetic benchmark released with \millet{} \citep{early2024millet}: 1{,}000
series (500 train / 500 test) of length 1{,}008 over 10 classes, in which a generator puts the class
signal inside a known window. It is the only dataset here with \emph{per-time-step ground truth}, so
it is the only place where we can score an interpretation instead of just looking at it, and RQ2 is
answered there. For wider coverage we use the \ucr{} archive \citep{dau2019ucr}: we report the
85-dataset subset used by \millet{}, so that the numbers can be compared, and the full 128-dataset
archive in \Cref{app:ucr}.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
Classification is test accuracy, and across \ucr{} also mean rank over the datasets that every model
in the comparison finished \citep{demsar2006statistical}, reported in \Cref{app:ucr}. Interpretation
is measured in two ways. \textbf{AOPCR} \citep{samek2017aopc} removes time steps in the order the
model says they are important and adds up the drop in the score of the predicted class; it needs no
ground truth, so we can use it everywhere, but it has no fixed scale --- a point \Cref{sec:res:aopcr}
comes back to. \textbf{\ndcg{}} \citep{jarvelin2002ndcg} ranks the time steps by the importance the
model claims and scores that ranking against the true signal window from the generator; it lies in
$[0,1]$ and needs ground truth, so it exists only on \webtraffic{}. \ndcg{} is the stronger of the
two and we treat it that way.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
\millet{} with its reference InceptionTime backbone \citep{fawaz2020inceptiontime} is the model this
paper is about; FCN and ResNet \citep{wang2017fcn} with the same head give two more reference points.
Because \Cref{sec:res:aopcr} shows that faithfulness numbers cannot be compared between different
training recipes, we retrain every baseline ourselves instead of copying published values, and we
report \millet{} twice: once under \emph{our} budget, which is the only fair comparison, and once
under \emph{its own} published 1{,}500-epoch recipe, which is the harder target.

\paragraph{Training setup.}
One setting is used for every model and every dataset, so that a difference between two rows is a
difference between two models: Adam \citep{kingma2015adam} at learning rate $1.25\times10^{-3}$,
weight decay $1.2\times10^{-4}$, at most 400 epochs with early stopping at patience 60, batch size
$\min(\lfloor n_{\mathrm{train}}/10 \rfloor, 16)$ floored at 2, label smoothing $0.13$, stratified
$80/20$ validation split when $n_{\mathrm{train}} \ge 100$. Encoders use $\dmodel = 64$ with 4 blocks
unless marked \emph{wide} ($\dmodel = 128$, 6 blocks), and $\kappa = 0.1$; the rest of the settings
are in \Cref{app:hparams}. Cost is measured separately at batch 32, length 1008, 10 classes on one
GPU: parameters counted directly, FLOPs counted by hand from the layer shapes for one series with one
multiply--add counted as two operations, and latency as the mean of 30 timed passes after 30 warm-up
passes that we throw away.

\paragraph{Two limitations to keep in mind.}
First, \webtraffic{} was also the set we used to choose the design, so \webtraffic{} results are
optimistic by design; \ucr{} was never used to pick anything. Second, the seed counts are uneven:
three models were run with three seeds (\Cref{tab:main}, column $S$) and the rest with one, because
we ran 72 encoder--head combinations over up to 129 datasets with a fixed amount of compute. The
three-seed standard deviations of our main model are $\pm 0.016$ for accuracy, $\pm 0.214$ for AOPCR
and $\pm 0.013$ for \ndcg{} (\Cref{app:seeds}), and anywhere in this paper we treat a gap smaller than
those as telling us nothing.
