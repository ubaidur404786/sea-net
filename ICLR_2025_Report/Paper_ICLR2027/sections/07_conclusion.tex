\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Multiple instance learning gives a time series classifier a prediction and a per-time-step
explanation from one forward pass, but the reference formulation carries a backbone one to two orders
of magnitude larger than the devices that most need both. We showed that the expense is the encoder
and not the interpretability, and that a bottlenecked separable dilated encoder with per-class
\mbox{Top-$k$} pooling gives a 41\,K-parameter, 76.7\,MFLOP model that beats the equally-trained
baseline at both tasks where ground truth exists, and trails it by two accuracy points across 85
\ucr{} datasets. Inherent interpretability is not what makes these models large, and does not have to
be given up to make them small.
