\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Multiple instance learning gives a time series classifier a prediction and a per-time-step
explanation from one forward pass, but the standard version carries a backbone ten to a hundred times
larger than what fits on the devices that need both the most. We showed that the cost is the encoder
and not the interpretability, and that a separable dilated encoder with a bottleneck and per-class
\mbox{Top-$k$} pooling gives a 41\,K-parameter, 76.7\,MFLOP model that beats the equally-trained
baseline at both jobs where ground truth exists, and stays two accuracy points behind it across 85
\ucr{} datasets. Built-in interpretability is not what makes these models large, and it does not have
to be given up to make them small.
