\section*{Reproducibility Statement}

Everything needed to reproduce the results is either in the paper or in the anonymous code release.
The models are defined in \Cref{sec:method} and, for the encoder and pooling variants not used in the
main text, in \Cref{app:encoders,app:heads}. The single training setting used for every model and
every dataset is listed in \Cref{sec:setup} and repeated with all the remaining hyperparameters in
\Cref{app:hparams}. The two evaluation metrics are defined in \Cref{sec:setup}, and the perturbation
procedure behind AOPCR, which \Cref{sec:res:aopcr} shows depends on the training recipe, is given in
\Cref{app:metrics}. \webtraffic{} is produced by the released generator of \citet{early2024millet} at
the settings in \Cref{app:hparams}; the \ucr{} archive \citep{dau2019ucr} is public and used with its
published train/test splits and no preprocessing other than standardising each series. Seed counts
are given per row in \Cref{tab:main} and the per-seed values are in \Cref{app:seeds}. Cost figures
come from the measurement procedure in \Cref{sec:setup}; the script that produces them is in the code
release. All 72 model combinations are listed row by row in \Cref{app:leaderboard}. Anonymous code,
configurations and result tables: \anonrepo{}.

\section*{Ethics Statement}

This work collects no new data and involves no human subjects. It uses the public \ucr{} archive
\citep{dau2019ucr} under its original terms and a synthetic benchmark produced by released code; some
\ucr{} datasets come from human physiological recordings, but they are distributed in anonymised form
by their maintainers and we did not try to re-identify them or add to them. The one risk worth naming
is specific to this topic: a model that shows an explanation makes people trust it more than one that
does not, and \Cref{sec:res:aopcr} shows that a common faithfulness score can be raised by training
longer rather than by explaining better. Interpretations from models of this kind should therefore be
checked on the target domain before they are used to support decisions about people. The code we
build on is released under the Apache 2.0 licence and its attribution notice is kept in our release.
