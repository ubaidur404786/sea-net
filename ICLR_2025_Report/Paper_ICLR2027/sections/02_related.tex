\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Deep time series classification.}
Convolutional models have been the strongest in this field since FCN and ResNet were shown to work
well without hand-made features \citep{wang2017fcn}, and InceptionTime \citep{fawaz2020inceptiontime}
is still the reference deep architecture on the \ucr{} archive \citep{dau2019ucr}; the survey of
\citet{fawaz2019review} covers the designs between them. Accuracy in this field is almost always
reported without a cost column, and the models are large: those three architectures use between
267\,K and 506\,K parameters at the input length we study.

\paragraph{Interpretability and multiple instance learning.}
Attribution methods \citep{ribeiro2016lime, lundberg2017shap, sundararajan2017ig} explain a trained
model afterwards; \citet{rudin2019stop} argues for building the explanation in instead, and for time
series VQShape \citep{wen2024vqshape} and InterpGN \citep{wen2025interpgn} do so with shapelet-like
tokens and gated expert models. MIL \citep{dietterich1997mil} gives a different way, in which the
interpretation is a value computed inside the network rather than a separate output. Attention-based
MIL \citep{ilse2018attention} and its dual-stream version \citep{li2021dsmil} developed the pooling
methods for histopathology, and \millet{} \citep{early2024millet} moved the method to time series
with \emph{conjunctive pooling}, the baseline we start from. Keeping only the strongest instances
instead of averaging all of them is also not new: WELDON \citep{durand2016weldon} introduced $k$-max
region selection for weakly supervised recognition, and the same idea appears again in weakly
supervised segmentation.

\paragraph{Small architectures and deployment budgets.}
Depthwise-separable convolution \citep{chollet2017xception, howard2017mobilenets} and
squeeze-and-expand bottlenecks \citep{he2016resnet, iandola2016squeezenet, sandler2018mobilenetv2}
are the two standard ways to reduce the parameter count of a convolutional network, and both carry
over directly to the 1-D case. For time series, ATCN \citep{baharani2022atcn} combines residual
connections with separable convolution for embedded inference, and \citet{risso2023tcn} search the
dilation and channel structure of TCNs under edge constraints; neither aims at interpretability ---
they produce a label, and where it came from is left open. On the deployment side, MCUNet
\citep{lin2020mcunet} and MicroNets \citep{banbury2021micronets} describe what fits on common
microcontrollers (flash for weights, SRAM for activations, operation count as a stand-in for latency)
and both find that architectures designed without those limits do not simply shrink into them.
Post-training integer quantisation \citep{jacob2018quantization} is the standard last step and cuts
weight memory by about four times. We use these budgets as the target the paper aims at, not as a
result: no measurement here was taken on a microcontroller.

\paragraph{Our contribution.}
Every part we use already exists. What is missing above is the combination: small time series
architectures do not produce interpretations, interpretable ones are not built to a cost budget, and
work on MIL pooling judges selection by bag-level accuracy rather than against per-instance ground
truth. This paper fills that gap. We contribute the per-class form of $k$-max selection inside
conjunctive pooling, with $k$ set as a fraction of the series length and with the exact return to the
baseline head at $\kappa = 1$ (\Cref{prop:topk}); its pairing with a bottlenecked encoder that keeps
the length unchanged; an ablation that assigns the size saving and the interpretation quality to the
two parts separately; and the finding that the faithfulness measure used in this field cannot be
compared across training budgets.
