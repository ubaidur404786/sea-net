Deep neural networks classify time series accurately, but they usually give only a
label and no reason for it. The \millet{} framework showed that Multiple Instance
Learning (MIL) pooling turns a standard backbone into a model that explains itself:
the network scores every time step, and those scores \emph{are} the explanation.
During this internship I built \seanet{}, \todo{one sentence: what \seanet{} is}.
My main contribution is \todo{one sentence: the new encoder family and the new
pooling heads}. I evaluated \num{N} model variants on the \webtraffic{} dataset and
on the 85 \ucr{} datasets used by \millet{}, measuring both classification quality
(accuracy, loss) and explanation quality (AOPCR, NDCG@$n$), over \num{S} random
seeds. My best variant reaches \num{web\_acc} accuracy on \webtraffic{} against
\num{millet\_acc} for my re-trained \millet{} baseline, while using \num{X}$\times$
fewer parameters and improving AOPCR from \num{a1} to \num{a2}.
