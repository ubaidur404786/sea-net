\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\subsection*{Summary of the work}

This internship asked whether a time series classifier can be made smaller \emph{and} more
interpretable at the same time, instead of trading one for the other. I built the \seanet{} encoder
family -- six multi-scale depthwise-separable TCN variants plus two wrappers
(\Cref{sec:architecture}) -- and seven Multiple Instance Learning pooling heads
(\Cref{sec:pooling}). I then compared 72 encoder/pooling combinations against FCN, ResNet and
\millet{} baselines that I re-trained myself with the same recipe, over \webtraffic{} and the
128-dataset \ucr{} archive (\Cref{sec:setup}).

The headline result is on \webtraffic{}. \texttt{seanet\_bottleneck\_topk} beats the re-trained
\millet{} baseline on accuracy, AOPCR and NDCG@$n$ at the same time, using 90\,\% fewer parameters
(41{,}324 against 423{,}707), a third of the file size and less time per prediction
(\Cref{tab:cost}). The best model of the
whole sweep, \texttt{seanet\_gated\_mean\_topk}, reaches 0.955 accuracy at 61{,}740 parameters, and
is the most stable model I trained (\Cref{tab:seeds}).

\subsection*{Objectives revisited}

\begin{itemize}\itemsep4pt
  \item \textbf{O1, reproduce the baselines -- Yes.} I selected \millet{} as the baseline and went
        further than the original criterion: FCN, ResNet and InceptionTime + Conjunctive were all
        re-trained by me with \seanet{}'s exact recipe, not just compared against published numbers
        (\Cref{sec:setup:baselines}).

  \item \textbf{O2, match the published \millet{} results -- Yes, and I can now say what the gap
        was.} Under \emph{my} recipe the re-trained \millet{} baseline scores 0.887 on
        \webtraffic{} and 0.8274 over the \ucr{} datasets, against a published \ucr{} mean of
        0.8445. Running \millet{}'s \emph{own} published recipe on my harness, with the identical
        architecture and code, lifts that to 0.920 on \webtraffic{} and \textbf{0.8434} over
        \ucr{} -- about one thousandth off the published number, with a balanced 26/32/26
        per-dataset record against it (\Cref{tab:ucr_accuracy}). So the architecture reproduces and
        the shortfall was the training budget, which is the controlled answer O2 asked for.

  \item \textbf{O3, design a smaller encoder, $\geq$50\,\% fewer parameters at equal accuracy --
        Yes.} \texttt{seanet\_bottleneck\_topk} beats the re-trained \millet{} baseline's accuracy
        at 41{,}324 parameters against 423{,}707 -- a 90\,\% reduction, well past the 50\,\% target
        (\Cref{tab:cost}).

  \item \textbf{O4, improve explanation quality -- Partly.} On \webtraffic{} both AOPCR and NDCG@$n$
        improve clearly over the re-trained baseline (\Cref{tab:web_main}), and the
        $\kappa$ ablation (\Cref{tab:ablation_topk}) shows the improvement comes from the mechanism
        I claimed rather than by accident. But the concern already noted in \Cref{tab:objectives}
        held up: over the 85 \ucr{} datasets my heads lose more datasets than they win
        (\Cref{sec:res:ucr}). So I count this as reached on \webtraffic{} specifically, not as a
        general claim.

  \item \textbf{O5, build a reproducible benchmark -- Yes.} Any encoder/pooling combination trains
        from one configuration file (\Cref{sec:setup:pipeline}), and every figure and table in this
        report is regenerated by one command from the stored results (\Cref{app:commands}).

  \item \textbf{O6, test on a tiny device -- No.} Not attempted. The cost numbers make the case that
        it should be feasible (\texttt{seanet\_bottleneck\_topk}: 41\,K parameters, 1.43\,MB,
        \Cref{tab:cost}), which is comparable in scale to the MLPerf Tiny reference model that
        motivated this objective \citep{banbury2021mlperftiny}. But a real on-device test, ideally
        after quantisation, is still open work.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{The limitation that matters most}

My headline result was selected on \webtraffic{} alone. It is the only dataset with per-time-step
ground truth, so it was the natural screening set (\Cref{sec:setup:data}), but \Cref{sec:res:ucr}
shows directly what that costs: over 85 very different \ucr{} datasets the re-trained baseline comes
out ahead on average. Every other limitation in \Cref{sec:disc:limitations} narrows what one number
means. This one narrows what the whole headline claim means, so it is the one I would put first if a
reader only had time for one.

\subsection*{Future work}

\begin{itemize}\itemsep4pt
  \item \textbf{Re-train \seanet{} under the long recipe.} Now that \millet{}'s own recipe is known
        to recover the published \ucr{} number on my harness (\Cref{tab:ucr_accuracy}), the obvious
        next experiment is to give \emph{my} encoders the same four-times-longer budget. It would
        say whether the \ucr{} gap in \Cref{sec:res:ucr} is really architectural or just the budget
        I could not afford across 72 variants.
  \item \textbf{Raise the dilation cap for long series.} \Cref{app:figures} shows accuracy falling
        with series length (correlation $-0.34$; 0.857 on the short half of the archive against
        0.755 on the long half), which is exactly what a fixed dilation cap of 16 would predict.
        Making the cap depend on the series length is a small change with a clear hypothesis behind
        it.
  \item \textbf{Fix the two heads that failed for an optimisation reason, not a capacity one.}
        Dual-stream pooling can represent the safe baseline but never finds it
        (\Cref{sec:disc:negative}). Starting its blend $\lambda$ near zero, or penalising it away
        from the safe branch early in training, is a direct test of that diagnosis.
  \item \textbf{Evaluate on a real dataset with per-time-step annotations.} \webtraffic{}'s ground
        truth is a generator's own record of what it changed. Checking explanation quality against
        annotations somebody actually made would be much stronger evidence
        (\Cref{sec:disc:limitations}).
  \item \textbf{Put the model on the device.} Quantise \texttt{seanet\_bottleneck\_topk} and measure
        accuracy, latency and memory on a real MCU, which is what objective O6 asked for and the
        natural next step for this project.
\end{itemize}
