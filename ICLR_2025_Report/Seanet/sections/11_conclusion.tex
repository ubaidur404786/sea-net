\subsection*{Summary of the work}

\todo{Three or four sentences: the problem, what I built (\seanet{} encoder family and
seven MIL pooling heads), how I evaluated it, and the headline number.}

{This internship asked whether a time-series classifier could be made smaller
\emph{and} more interpretable at the same time, instead of trading one for the other.
I built the \seanet{} encoder family -- six multi-scale depthwise-separable TCN
variants (\Cref{sec:architecture}) -- and seven Multiple Instance Learning pooling
heads (\Cref{sec:pooling}), and compared 66 encoder/pooling combinations against
re-trained FCN, ResNet and \millet{} baselines over \webtraffic{} and the 128-dataset
\ucr{} archive (\Cref{sec:setup}). The headline number:
\texttt{seanet\_bottleneck\_topk} matches or beats the re-trained \millet{} baseline
on accuracy, AOPCR and NDCG@$n$ on \webtraffic{} at once, using 90\% fewer parameters
(41{,}324 vs.\ 423{,}707, \Cref{tab:cost}); the single best model in the sweep,
\texttt{seanet\_gated\_mean\_topk}, reaches the top accuracy of all 66 variants at
4.4$\times$ fewer parameters than the equal-accuracy alternative
(\Cref{fig:pareto}).}

\subsection*{Objectives revisited}

\todo{One short paragraph or a five-line list going through O1--O5 of
\Cref{tab:objectives}, each with a clear yes / partly / no and the evidence.}

\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
  \item \textbf{O1, reproduce all the baselines -- Yes.} I selected \millet{} as the
        baseline (\Cref{tab:objectives}) and went further than the original
        criterion: FCN, ResNet and InceptionTime+Conjunctive were all re-trained
        myself with \seanet{}'s exact recipe, not just compared against published
        numbers (\Cref{sec:setup:baselines}).
  \item \textbf{O2, match published \millet{} results -- Partly.} The original
        criterion compared a single number (89\% vs.\ a published 0.940,
        \Cref{tab:objectives}) and was left open pending a reply from the \millet{}
        authors. With the fuller comparison now available: my re-trained \millet{}
        baseline scores 0.8873 on \webtraffic{} and 0.8274 mean accuracy on the
        85-dataset \ucr{} archive, against a published \ucr{} mean of 0.8445
        (\Cref{tab:ucr_accuracy}) -- close, but I have not yet run
        \texttt{configs/models/sv1/millet\_paper.yaml} (their exact recipe on my
        harness) to separate a recipe difference from an architecture one
        (\Cref{sec:disc:reading}), so I am not calling this fully reached.
  \item \textbf{O3, design a smaller encoder, $\geq$50\% fewer params at equal
        accuracy -- Yes.} \texttt{seanet\_bottleneck\_topk} matches or beats the
        re-trained \millet{} baseline's accuracy at 41{,}324 params against 423{,}707
        -- a 90\% reduction, well past the 50\% target (\Cref{tab:cost}), consistent
        with the ``10 times smaller'' the objective originally aimed for
        (\Cref{tab:objectives}).
  \item \textbf{O4, improve explanation quality -- Partly.} On \webtraffic{}, both
        AOPCR and NDCG@$n$ improve over the re-trained baseline
        (\Cref{tab:explanation_quality}), matching the original criterion exactly.
        But \Cref{tab:objectives}'s own note that it ``struggles on some datasets''
        held up under the full \ucr{} sweep: on the 85-dataset archive, both proposed
        heads lose more datasets than they win against the re-trained baseline
        (\Cref{sec:res:ucr}), so I call this reached on \webtraffic{} specifically,
        not as a general claim.
  \item \textbf{O5, build a reproducible benchmark -- Yes.} Any encoder/pooling
        combination trains from one YAML file (\Cref{sec:setup:pipeline}), and every
        figure and table in this report is regenerated by one command
        (\texttt{python main.py paper}, \Cref{app:commands}) from the stored
        results, matching the objective's own criterion exactly.
  \item \textbf{O6, test on a tiny device -- No.} Not yet attempted, as
        \Cref{tab:objectives} already states. The cost numbers make the case that it
        should be feasible (\texttt{seanet\_bottleneck\_topk}: 37.7M FLOPs, 1.43\,MB,
        \Cref{tab:cost}) and comparable in scale to the MLPerf Tiny reference model
        that motivated this objective (Section~1, \citealp{banbury2021mlperftiny}),
        but a real on-device test -- ideally after quantisation, as MLPerf Tiny's own
        baseline does -- is still open work.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Limitations}

\todo{One or two sentences, consistent with \Cref{sec:disc:limitations}. Do not repeat
the whole list -- name the one limitation that matters most.}

{The one limitation that matters most is that my headline result was selected on
\webtraffic{} alone: it is the only dataset with per-time-step ground truth, so it
was the natural screening set (\Cref{sec:setup:data}), but \Cref{sec:res:ucr} shows
directly that a model chosen this way does not automatically generalise to 85 very
different \ucr{} datasets, where the re-trained baseline actually comes out ahead on
average. Every other limitation in \Cref{sec:disc:limitations} (synthetic data,
seed count, univariate-only, no human study) narrows what a given number means; this
one narrows what the whole headline claim means.}
